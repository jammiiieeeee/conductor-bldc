# bldc-motor

Mode: read. Audience: learners with no mechanical vocabulary, at a desk, one pass.

## Direction contract

THESIS: The motor is not nine parts illustrated beside each other; it is one axial stack you pull apart with your hand, and the only saturated thing on the page is the copper that carries current. Refuses the category's dark HUD with a neon wireframe — the future here is exactness, not glow.

OWN-WORLD: Granite-grey lapped surface plate (#E7EAEB) with a 24px hairline grid held at 3% ink; near-black cool ink (#14181A); secondary grey (#596265); one accent, copper (#A8471E for text, #C0703F for strokes), used only where electricity flows. Type: Archivo variable at expanded widths for display and normal width for reading; Azeret Mono for numerals, part indices and measured readouts only. Every line is a single hairline weight; no boxes, no shadows except a soft contact shadow under each exploded part.

STORY: The reader explodes the stack, selects a part, reads what it is and what it does, then understands the gap section: current makes a rotating field, the magnets chase it, the shaft carries the difference. They leave able to name all nine parts and say why the rotor turns.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Full-bleed granite field. Masthead rule across the top: "BLDC / 12N14P" left, three section links right, live explode percentage far right. Left column at 34%: display headline "Twelve coils, fourteen magnets, one air gap." at ~clamp(3rem,5vw,5rem) expanded 110% width, tight tracking; a short deck; a four-item spec row in mono; the explode control (native range input, copper track, mono readout); below it the part readout plate, which swaps with the selected part. Right column at 66%: the motor drawn in axonometric, closed and slowly turning at 0%, opening into nine floating parts as the control moves, with numbered badges riding each part and a nine-button part rail beneath the drawing. Background grid stops before the text column.

FORM: Assigned direction 7 of 7 from the grounded list — the motor-winding instruction sheet: one continuous conductor traced through a sequence of numbered stations. Seed: 45f0f86e34e03daed65135daa088f1fe. Materials translated for the pinned futuristic/minimal/techy brief; the topology, the trace, and the numbered-station ritual are kept.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance.

## Weighing

- Challenger 1 (iridescent cloud edge): declined. Lends the agent one discipline — a single active band rendered as the only colour on an achromatic field. Kept as: copper is the one band; everything else is grey.
- Challenger 2 (brick build instructions): declined. Lends step-numbering that aligns to a module grid. Kept as: numerals sit on the parts, not in a legend.
- Challenger 3 (cutting bench select rail): declined. Lends state-as-a-mark rather than colour. Kept as: selected part thickens its outline and lifts; nothing changes hue.
- Challenger 4 (cracktro queue): declined on both axes — unreadable for learners, and a dark screen for a daylight desk.
- Challenger 5 (patent sheet): competitive, held audience identification only. Its leader-line discipline is the reason the numerals ride the parts.
- Challenger 6 (Bauhaus workshop): declined. Lends composition rebalancing rather than shrinking. Kept as: the drawing pulls the camera back as it explodes.
